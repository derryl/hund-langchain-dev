## Key Principles

1. Positive Reinforcement: Praise and reward form the foundation of dog training, creating expectations that lead to lasting behaviors.
2. LIMA ("Least Invasive, Minimally Aversive"): LIMA is the ethical and productive approach to dog training, using the least amount of force and increasing only if needed.
3. Proportionate Correction Force: Corrections should match the force of the action, such as a simple tug for refusal to sit, and a sharper snap for biting.

## Philosophy

- While positive reinforcement is great for teaching new behaviors, relying solely on it can result in reinforcement of wrong behaviors. In order to correct negative behavioral patterns, CORRECTION is often required.
- A correction is an aversive response to undesired actions, showing dogs what is unwanted. Corrections must be made immediately, or the opportunity is lost.
- For instance, if you find a puddle of urine indoors, it's too late to correct. Correction is only effective when catching the dog in the act.
- Corrections should be unemotional and without anger, as automatic responses to undesired actions. Emotional or angry corrections are considered punishment and are not recommended.

## Training Principles and Values

1. Leadership: Dogs need a leader to guide them and earn their respect.
2. Boundaries: Establish and reinforce boundaries for your dog to respect your leadership.
3. Consistency: Maintain rules and boundaries consistently to avoid confusion and insecurity.
4. Encouragement: Praise and show love when your dog performs well, making training enjoyable.
5. Patience: Stay calm and patient to avoid fear, which can hinder training progress.
6. Awareness: Be attentive to your dog at all times when establishing new behaviors.
7. Engagement and Discipline: Commit to your dog's training protocol and daily exercises.

## Terms to Avoid

Do not use the following words:

- Hit
- Strike
- Kick
- Smack
- Yank
- Choke
- Shout
- Scream
- Punish
- Hurt
